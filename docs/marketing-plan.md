# Stocked — Solo-Operator Marketing Plan to 100 Customers

*Written July 2026. One operator (founder), ~15–20 hrs/week on go-to-market alongside product work.*

---

## 1. Positioning

**One sentence:** Stocked is inventory, tools, and purchasing for small field-service and construction teams — track what you buy, where it went, and which job consumed it, from a phone.

**Why we win against the incumbents:**

| Competitor | Their gap | Our wedge |
|---|---|---|
| Sortly | No POs/receiving, no job costing | Full purchase → receive → consume-to-job loop |
| inFlow / Zoho Inventory | Built for resellers/e-commerce | Built for *consuming* inventory on jobs (requisitions, job codes, cost centers) |
| Spreadsheets (the real competitor) | No accountability, no live counts | Stock counts with attendee sign-off, audit trail, mobile scanning |

**Pricing story (already live):** every plan has every feature; you pay by team size. Starter $49/mo (5 users), Pro $99/mo (20 users), 14-day free trial. This is a marketing asset — "no feature hostage-taking" — say it everywhere.

**Beachhead ICP (pick ONE for the first 50 customers):**
Contractors / field-service companies, 5–50 employees, with a warehouse or van stock, currently on spreadsheets or paper requisition slips. Owner or ops manager buys. Pain trigger: materials going missing, jobs over budget, year-end count chaos.

---

## 2. Funnel math (what 100 customers requires)

Working backwards, with honest solo-founder assumptions:

- Trial → paid conversion: **35%** (achievable because you'll onboard every trial personally at this scale)
- Monthly churn: **3%** → need ~**112 gross** customers to net 100
- Trials needed: **~320** over 12 months (~27/month average, back-loaded)
- Where trials come from: ~40% direct outreach, ~30% content/SEO, ~20% directories/reviews, ~10% referrals

The point of the phases below is to hit those trial numbers without paid ads until the funnel is proven.

---

## 3. Phase 0 — Foundation (Weeks 1–2, before any promotion)

1. **Three design partners.** Give 3 real businesses in the ICP free Pro for 6 months in exchange for: weekly feedback calls (first month), a testimonial, and a case study with numbers ("cut monthly stock count from 2 days to 3 hours"). These case studies power everything else.
2. **Sharpen the landing page for the ICP.** Right now it sells "inventory software"; retitle the hero to the beachhead: *"Stop losing materials between the warehouse and the job."* Add the case studies as soon as they exist.
3. **Set up tracking.** UTM on every link, a simple spreadsheet (or the app's own dashboard) logging: source → visit → trial → activated (created first PO or count) → paid. You cannot run this plan without knowing which channel converts.
4. **Record a 3-minute demo video** (Loom is fine): receive a PO on your phone → issue materials to a job → run a stock count. Pin it on the landing page and reuse in every outreach message.

---

## 4. Phase 1 — Founder-led sales (Months 1–3 → first 10 customers)

Goal: 10 paying customers, and *learning which message converts*. Everything is manual on purpose.

**Direct outreach — the core motion (45–60 min/day, 4 days/week):**
- Build a list of 300 ICP companies (Google Maps, local contractor associations, Facebook trade groups, LinkedIn). 15–20 new contacts/day.
- Channel by geography: email + LinkedIn for overseas; **Facebook Messenger/Viber and walk-ins work better for PH/SEA local businesses** — adapt to where your list lives.
- Script (keep it this short): *"Do your crews still write material requests on paper? I built a phone app where they request, you approve, and stock deducts automatically — it also catches count shortages. 15-min demo this week? Here's a 3-min video: [link]"*
- Expect: ~5% book a demo, ~40% of demos start trials, ~50% of hand-held trials convert → **300 contacts ≈ 3 customers.** You'll need ~1,000 contacts over the phase; that's the job.

**Communities (30 min/day, permanent habit):**
- Join 5–8 places the ICP hangs out: contractor Facebook groups, r/Construction, r/smallbusiness, local trade associations. **Answer inventory/job-costing questions helpfully for 2 weeks before ever mentioning the product.** Then mention it only when directly relevant, with the demo video.

**Every trial gets white-glove onboarding:** personally import their product list (the CSV import you built), set up their locations, and do one live stock count with them. At this stage onboarding *is* marketing — it's what makes 35% conversion real.

**Exit criteria for Phase 1:** 10 customers, one repeatable message that books demos, 2 written case studies.

---

## 5. Phase 2 — Build compounding channels (Months 3–6 → 35 customers)

Keep outreach running at half intensity; add assets that generate trials while you sleep.

**Directory listings (one-time setup, week 1 of phase):**
- Free listings on Capterra, GetApp, Software Advice, G2, SourceForge, and AlternativeTo (list as Sortly/inFlow alternative). 
- Get every existing customer to leave a review (ask right after a success moment, e.g. their first clean stock count). 10+ reviews on Capterra materially changes inbound.

**SEO content engine (one piece/week, ~4 hrs — this is the biggest compounding lever):**
- *Comparison pages first* (highest intent): "Sortly vs Stocked", "inFlow alternative for contractors", "Zoho Inventory vs Stocked for field teams".
- *Template pages second* (volume + goodwill): free downloadable "stock count sheet", "material requisition form", "tool checkout log" — each with a "or do this automatically" CTA into the trial.
- *Job-costing content third*: "how to track material costs per job", "construction inventory management guide". 
- Expect near-zero traffic for 8–10 weeks, then compounding. By month 6 this should produce 5–10 trials/month on its own.

**YouTube (one 5–10 min video every 2 weeks):** screen-recorded how-tos matching the articles ("How to run a warehouse stock count with your phone"). YouTube is dramatically underrated for boring B2B software and doubles as your demo library.

**Accountant/bookkeeper partnerships (5 emails/week):** your accounting-export + journal feed is the hook. Offer bookkeepers who serve contractors: free account for their practice + 20% first-year referral fee. One good bookkeeper = 3–10 customers.

**Exit criteria:** 35 customers, ≥30% of new trials from non-outreach sources, trial→paid still ≥30%.

---

## 6. Phase 3 — Double down + referrals (Months 6–12 → 100 customers)

- **Kill/scale review:** drop the weakest channel, double the strongest (your tracking sheet decides — not vibes).
- **Referral program in-app:** give a month free to both sides. At this stage every customer knows other contractors; make asking automatic (prompt after 60 days of active use).
- **Case-study flywheel:** one new customer story per month, cross-posted to the blog, LinkedIn, and the communities.
- **Optional paid ($300–500/mo only if unit economics are proven):** Google Ads on high-intent terms only ("inventory app for contractors", "Sortly alternative"), and Capterra PPC. At $64 average revenue/customer/month and 3% churn, a customer's lifetime value is ~$2,100 — you can afford ~$150–300 CAC comfortably, but only spend once organic conversion data proves the funnel.
- **Raise Enterprise conversations:** by now a few 20+ user prospects will have appeared; hand-negotiate those (this is where the forecast gets upside).

**Weekly operating cadence (the whole plan on one calendar):**

| Day | Block (~2–3 hrs) |
|---|---|
| Mon | Write/publish one content piece; schedule social posts |
| Tue | Outreach: 20 contacts + follow-ups |
| Wed | Demos + trial onboarding calls |
| Thu | Outreach: 20 contacts; community participation |
| Fri | Metrics review; customer check-ins; ask for one review/referral |

**Metrics to watch weekly:** trials started (by source), activation rate (created a PO or count in week 1), trial→paid, MRR, churn. Rule of thumb: if trial→paid drops below 25%, stop adding traffic and fix onboarding.

## 8. What this plan deliberately does NOT include

- **Paid ads before month 7** — you can't out-spend incumbents; you can out-help them.
- **Lifetime deals (AppSumo etc.)** — fast users, terrible revenue, support burden that kills a solo operator. Revisit only if you want testers, not customers.
- **A second ICP before customer 50** — the temptation will be constant (clinics! restaurants! warehouses!). The suite vision (CrewSync, JobLedger) makes construction/field-service the strategic beachhead: Stocked's job-costing feed is the moat there.
